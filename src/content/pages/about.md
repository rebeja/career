My career began in the classroom. I spent a decade designing courses and teaching communication at the University of Iowa and St. Edward's University before moving into technical writing at Indeed. That training never went away. I still start every project with the same question a teacher asks: what does this learner need, in this moment, to succeed?

Today I apply that question to developer documentation, partner onboarding, and AI tooling. The answer is usually a system, not a page.

## How I work

### I build the system, not just the content
Templates, intake workflows, contribution guides, quality rubrics, and automation pipelines keep running after I've moved on. The installation guide workflow I designed in 2022 let another writer scale production on their own. The Indeed University curriculum I built ran without me the following year. I measure my work by what keeps going without me.

### I design for the reader's situation
My graduate training is in rhetoric, the study of how a message meets a specific audience with a specific need. In documentation, that means structuring content around what the reader is trying to do: understand a concept, look up a definition, or follow a procedure. Good information architecture works like a good syllabus. It moves people from orientation to independence in the right order.

### I treat AI as infrastructure
I use Claude Code every day as a pair-programming partner, and I've built production AI tools for quality scoring, release notes, and Japanese localization. I approached the Doc Analyzer the way a professor designs a grading rubric, then trained the LLM to apply it as my teaching assistant. The same discipline applies to governance: codified prompts, terminology, guardrails, and human review gates so that machine-generated content meets the same standard as anything a person writes.

### I teach others to do the same
The most useful thing I can do with a new capability is make it someone else's. That's why I run demos, write playbooks, add shared rules to the repo, and designed my Document360 webinar as a starter kit rather than a presentation.

## Why it matters to me

Documentation decides who gets access. When a system is intelligible, more people (and now more AI agents) can build on it. I believe AI is changing how humans learn and work with knowledge, and that the ethics of that change matter. Transformation deserves to be done with care.
